# Rebuffer: two screens to design (M4.6, group B)

Rebuffer is a browser-only lab for HLS video: a real player (hls.js) plays a stream through a simulated network,
live charts show what it does, and a report scores the run. Live at https://rebuffer.azamatos.com. This project
already holds the app's look: its colour tokens in both themes, its fonts, its compiled stylesheet and 14 of its
parts (buttons, cards, chips, tables, the Chart | Table switch, menus, toasts and more). Design with those parts.

## Limits for every screen

- **Both themes:** the dark "instrument panel" (the default) and the light one. Text contrast at least 4.5:1, large
  text and controls at least 3:1.
- **Keyboard first:** every control is reachable with Tab and shows its focus. Nothing is shown only on hover.
  Every chart keeps its Chart | Table switch.
- **Widths:** desktop up to 1280 px and phones from 360 px, with no sideways page scroll.
- **Style budget:** the whole app's stylesheet must stay under 8 KB compressed, and about 100 bytes are left.
  Reuse the existing parts, colours, spacing and corner radii. Avoid new colours, shadows, gradients and one-off
  sizes; every new one costs bytes. If a design needs more, say so, and we'll decide on a bigger budget.
- **Words:** plain and short, numbers with their units, no streaming jargon without a few words of explanation.
- **Compare's colours today:** run A is a solid line in the text colour, run B a dashed line in the accent. Two new
  hues are possible if you think they read better.

## Screen 1: Inspect, for big streams

Inspect reads an HLS stream's playlists and lists what's wrong with them.

**Today:**

- A summary row with the counts of errors, warnings and notes, plus "Run this stream".
- A "Quality ladder" table with one row per variant: picture size and frame rate, declared bandwidth, measured peak,
  codecs.
- Below it, "Findings" on the left and a "Segments" panel on the right, with a playlist chooser of one button per
  playlist.

**The real case:** Apple's advanced example,
https://devstreaming-cdn.apple.com/videos/streaming/examples/adv_dv_atmos/main.m3u8.

- **Its size:** 100 variants, 10 audio playlists and 13 subtitle playlists, up to 2160p at 39 Mbps.
- **Its formats:** H.264, HEVC, Dolby Vision and HDR10+ ladders.
- **Shared playlists:** many variants use one video playlist with different audio; the 1080p `hls290` has three
  audio pairings.
- **What goes wrong today:** the ladder table runs to 100 rows, and the chooser becomes a wall of buttons. The
  Segments column grows far longer than Findings, leaving a tall empty space beside it.

**Design:**

1. **A playlist chooser that groups.** Video by codec and range (H.264, HEVC, Dolby Vision, HDR10+), then Audio, then
   Subtitles. Above about 12 playlists it becomes compact, for example a select with groups, or a pattern you prefer.
2. **Variants that share a video playlist are listed once,** with their audio pairings shown.
3. **The ladder table groups the same way.** One rendition must still be easy to find.
4. **A layout where Findings and Segments don't leave a large empty column.**
5. **States to show:**
   - Our own small stream (5 qualities, 1 audio, 1 subtitle track) still looks right.
   - Measuring, while the peaks load.
   - A pasted playlist whose links can't be read.

## Screen 2: Compare, reworked to answer a question

The owner's words: "we need real comparison which will be useful".

**Today:**

- Two run cards, A and B.
- A "What changed" table of the settings that differ.
- "The numbers": each metric for A and B, plus "B against A" in words with a verdict.
- The charts with both runs overlaid: buffer, quality and downloads.

The metrics are QoE score (out of 100), First frame, Stall time, Stalls, Rebuffer ratio, Average bitrate, Quality
switches, At top quality and Wasted data.

**Example data,** invented for the mock-up but shaped like two real runs of our film *Tears of Steel* on the
"Train through a tunnel" network, which scored 0 and 49:

- **A, "hls.js defaults":** scored 0. First frame 3.1 s, 2 stalls lasting 6.4 s, average 1.2 Mbps, 7 switches.
- **B, "Fast start":** scored 49. First frame 1.4 s, no stalls, average 1.9 Mbps, 4 switches.

**Design:**

1. **"Change one thing", starting from a report.**
   - The Report gets an action for it. The person picks one thing to change: another player preset, or another
     network.
   - The lab runs B with only that change, then opens Compare.
   - Design these steps: the entry point on the Report, the chooser, the Run screen while B runs (it should say it's
     the second half of a comparison), and arriving at Compare.
2. **A warning** when the two runs differ in more than one setting, or in the stream. Two different ladders can't
   share one quality axis, so their charts show Mbps only.
3. **A verdict headline first,** for example "B started 1.7 s sooner and never stalled; A stalled twice". Then the
   metric cards with A/B bars, grouped as Start, Smoothness, Picture and Data.
4. **Each chart gets one line on what to look for** and a takeaway from the data, for example "B stayed at 576p; A
   dropped to 342p at 0:12". When neither run stalled, the stall lanes are replaced by a single "No stalls in either
   run".
5. **States to show:**
   - Two runs with the same setup, where the only difference is the spread between runs: say so.
   - A run that was stopped early.
   - A phone width.
